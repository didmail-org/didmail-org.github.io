01 / SECURITY STARTS WITH IDENTITY

An address is public.
Access is personal.

Knowing where to reach you should not give anyone the means to become you.

01

Beyond an address and password.

Password-based mail accounts can be targeted by guessing, credential stuffing and phishing. DIDMail uses keys for identity and signatures for authorized operations; knowing an address and guessing a server login password is not enough.

02

A local password. Keys in your care.

The local password unlocks protected wallet data on your device. It is not a shared mailbox password sent to a provider. Strong passwords, safe backups and secure devices still matter: a stolen key can compromise the identity.

03

Encrypt before sending.

Message bodies and attachments are encrypted on the client for the recipient. Mail nodes handle ciphertext; decryption happens with the appropriate private keys on the client.

Peer-to-peer encryption. A network of services.

The cryptographic endpoints are the sender and recipient, even when a server relays the message. DID identity is separate from a single provider account; users can choose mail nodes, while ArcBlock on-chain assets and records support verifiable coordination. Decentralization does not mean a serverless transport or a copy of every message on every node.

Revenue depends on usage, pricing, protocol fee allocation and running costs. Returns are not guaranteed; test-network tokens are not cash income.

A FEW THINGS TO KNOW

The details
that matter.

Can knowing my address unlock my inbox?＋

An address identifies you; it is not a decryption key. Reading encrypted content requires the appropriate private key. Keep local wallet data, passwords and backups secure.

Is it free for an enterprise?＋

The original software has no license fee under Apache-2.0, including commercial use and self-hosting subject to the license terms. Hosting, operations and service or chain fees are separate. Third-party components retain their own licenses.

Is this direct, server-free delivery?＋

No. The encryption endpoints are the sender and recipient. Nodes still store and relay ciphertext. Delivery and availability depend on the selected services; encryption does not hide all routing, payment or on-chain metadata.

Can I use it today?＋

Public downloads and deployment instructions are being prepared. This site introduces the project and its architecture; the illustrations are concept studies. Follow GitHub for source and release updates.
